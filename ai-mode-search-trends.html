How AI Is Changing the Way We Search: Inside Google's AI Mode Trends

By Digital Fattom · AI Tools & Search Trends

Type a question into Google today and there's a good chance you're no longer typing a short keyword. You're asking something closer to a real question — the kind you'd ask a knowledgeable friend. That shift is not a small design update. It's the biggest change in how search works since Google Search itself was built.

Google's own research, published in its "How People Are Using AI Mode" report, confirms what many of us have already noticed in our own search bar: people aren't searching, they're conversing. Here's what the data actually shows, and what it means for students, professionals, and anyone building something online in Pakistan.

From Keywords to Conversations

For nearly two decades, "good searching" meant typing short, precise keywords — three or four words, stripped of grammar, built for a search engine rather than a human. That habit is fading fast. Google reports that the average AI Mode query now runs about three times longer than a traditional search query, and the most common opening words are "what," "how," "I," "is," and "can."

People are no longer searching for "running shoes flat feet." They're typing something closer to: "I have flat feet and my knees hurt — can you help me find running shoes that won't make it worse?"

That single change matters enormously for anyone creating content, running a small business, or teaching online. Search is becoming personal, contextual, and narrated — not just a lookup tool.

The Numbers Behind the Shift

A year after launch, AI Mode has crossed a billion monthly active users worldwide, and the number of queries has more than doubled every quarter since it began. A few more findings stand out:

  • Multimodal search is rising fast — more than 1 in 6 AI Mode searches in the U.S. now involve something other than plain text, such as a photo, voice input, or live video, and image-based searches are growing over 40% month-over-month.
  • Planning queries are booming — searches related to planning trips, workouts, meals, and budgets are growing 80% faster than AI Mode queries overall.
  • People are using AI to decide, not just discover — queries starting with "which" are growing 40% faster than the overall pace, suggesting people now use AI Mode at the final stage of a decision, comparing two specific options rather than just browsing.
  • Brainstorming is a real use case — creative and idea-generation queries are growing roughly 30% faster than the platform average.

Google groups this behavior into five broad categories: Explore, Learn, Decide, Create, and Do. In other words, AI Mode isn't just answering questions anymore — it's helping people plan trips, choose products, learn new skills, and get everyday tasks done, start to finish, inside one conversation.

What This Means for Content Creators and Small Businesses

If you run a blog, a Gumroad store, or a small brand — this shift changes the rules of visibility. A few practical takeaways:

  • Write like you're answering a real question, not stuffing in a keyword. Long, natural, conversational headings now match how people actually search.
  • Comparison content has real value. Since "which one" style searches are growing fast, articles that clearly compare two products, tools, or approaches are more likely to get pulled into an AI answer.
  • Product details need to be crawlable and clear. For online sellers, price, availability, color, and brand are the top details AI Mode looks for when helping someone shop — so listings with vague or missing details risk being skipped entirely.
  • Visual content matters more, not less. With image-based search growing sharply, clear product photos and well-described visuals are becoming part of SEO, not just presentation.
"AI Mode isn't replacing search — it's expanding what people believe is searchable at all."

The Trade-Off: Visibility Without Clicks

There's a catch worth understanding honestly. As AI answers get better at satisfying a question directly inside the search results, fewer users click through to the original website. Industry data suggests that when an AI-generated overview appears, top-ranking pages can lose a large share of the clicks they used to get, and a meaningful share of users end their search the moment they see the summary.

For publishers and small content businesses, that means the old goal — "get the click" — is being joined by a new one: "get cited, get recognized, get remembered as the trustworthy source." Being the brand or writer an AI system references by name is starting to carry weight, even when it doesn't always translate into a website visit.

Why This Matters Beyond SEO

This isn't only a marketing story. For students, AI-powered search is becoming a genuine study companion — one that explains a concept, then lets you ask a follow-up, then another, without starting a new search each time. For everyday users, it's becoming a decision-support tool for health questions, budgeting, travel, and shopping. For small business owners in Pakistan, it's a signal that showing up clearly, honestly, and in detail online matters more than ever — because AI systems favor sources that are easy to understand and hard to misread.

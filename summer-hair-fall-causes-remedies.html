Summer Hair Fall: Causes, Scalp Problems and Natural Remedies to Control It

By Fouzia Asim · November 2026 · 8 min read

Most people expect hair fall in winter. Then summer arrives, and the brush fills up even faster. Hair feels sticky by noon, the scalp itches, and every wash leaves more strands in the drain. If this sounds familiar, you are not imagining it. Summer hair fall is common in Pakistan, where heat, sweat, dust and strong sun put real pressure on the scalp. The good news is that most of it can be controlled once you understand the cause.

Is Some Hair Fall Normal?

Yes. Losing roughly 50 to 100 hairs a day is normal, because every hair goes through a cycle of growth, rest and shedding. Some people also notice a seasonal increase in shedding when the season changes, including from summer into autumn. It becomes a concern when you see far more hair than usual for several weeks, a widening parting, thin patches, or hair that falls out with a painful, red scalp.

Main Causes of Hair Fall in Summer

1. Sweat and oil build-up

The scalp produces more oil in hot weather, and sweat mixes with it along with dust and pollution. This layer clogs the openings around hair roots and creates a perfect place for scalp fungus to grow. Weak, clogged roots shed more easily.

2. Sun and UV damage

Ultraviolet rays damage hair the same way they damage skin. The outer layer of the strand roughens, the hair turns dry and brittle, and it snaps while combing. This looks like hair fall but is actually breakage.

3. Dehydration

We sweat a lot in summer and often drink too little water. A dehydrated body delivers fewer nutrients to the hair, which becomes dull and weak.

4. Fungal scalp problems and dandruff

Heat and humidity encourage a yeast called Malassezia, which lives naturally on most scalps. When it multiplies, it can cause itching, greasy yellowish flakes and inflammation, known as seborrheic dermatitis. An inflamed scalp holds hair less firmly. Many people mistake these sticky flakes for dryness and apply more oil, which can make the problem worse.

5. Swimming, chlorine and salt water

Pool water and sea water strip natural oils and leave hair rough and tangled. Hair left unrinsed after swimming breaks more easily.

6. Heat styling and tight hairstyles

Blow dryers, straighteners and curling irons add extra heat on top of already hot weather. Tight ponytails and buns on a sweaty scalp pull on the roots and can cause traction hair loss over time.

7. Poor eating in hot weather

Many people eat lighter or skip meals in summer. Low iron, vitamin D, protein and zinc are all linked with increased shedding. Crash dieting can trigger noticeable shedding a couple of months later.

8. Hidden health reasons

Thyroid problems, anaemia, PCOS, recent illness, high fever and stress can cause hair loss in any season. If shedding continues after you fix your routine, get these checked.

Dry scalp or oily dandruff? Small, dry, white flakes with tight skin usually mean dryness. Larger, yellowish, greasy flakes with itching and redness usually point to dandruff. Do not oil an itchy, sticky scalp; treat the dandruff first.

Natural Remedies That Help

Aloe vera and tea tree scalp mask

Mix two tablespoons of fresh aloe vera gel with three to four drops of pure tea tree oil. Massage gently into the scalp with your fingertips, leave for 20 minutes, then wash with a mild shampoo. Use once or twice a week. Tea tree oil has antifungal properties, but it must never be applied undiluted.

Apple cider vinegar rinse

After shampooing, pour a mug of clean water mixed with one to two tablespoons of raw apple cider vinegar over the scalp, massage for a minute or two, then rinse with plain water. It helps remove build-up and keeps the scalp slightly acidic. Use once a week, and skip it if your scalp is broken or burning.

Amla for strength

Amla (Indian gooseberry) is rich in vitamin C and has been used in hair care for generations. Eat a fresh amla or a spoon of amla powder in water daily, and use amla paste or amla-infused oil on the lengths of the hair. If your scalp is oily or flaky, keep oils off the scalp and apply only to the ends.

Rice water or fenugreek rinse

Soak a handful of rice or a spoon of fenugreek (methi) seeds in water for a few hours, strain, and use the liquid as a final rinse. It adds softness and shine and reduces tangles, which means less breakage. Rinse well so nothing stays on the scalp.

Be honest with yourself about results. Home remedies can improve scalp condition and reduce breakage, but they do not reverse medical hair loss. Always patch test on your inner arm 24 hours before using a new ingredient, especially essential oils, vinegar or lemon.

Daily Habits to Control Hair Fall in Summer

  1. Wash on time. If you sweat daily, wash the scalp every second day, or daily if needed, with a mild, sulfate-free shampoo. Use fingertips, not nails.
  2. Do not oil an oily or flaky scalp. Pause heavy oils until the itching settles.
  3. Protect from the sun. Wear a loose cotton scarf, cap or umbrella at midday.
  4. Rinse after swimming. Wet hair with clean water before entering the pool and wash it soon after.
  5. Give heat tools a rest. Let hair air-dry. If you must use a dryer, use a cool setting and keep it at a distance.
  6. Loosen your hairstyle. Avoid tight, sweaty ponytails and buns.
  7. Keep combs and pillow covers clean. Wash your comb weekly in warm soapy water and change pillow covers a few times a week. Do not share brushes.
  8. Trim the ends every two to three months.

What to Eat and Drink

  • Water: sip steadily through the day, and more when you are outdoors. Add lemon water, buttermilk (lassi without sugar) or coconut water.
  • Protein: eggs, lentils (daal), chickpeas, yogurt, chicken and fish support hair structure.
  • Iron and vitamin C: green leafy vegetables, dates and amla with citrus fruit help your body use iron.
  • Healthy fats: nuts, seeds and olive oil.
  • Cut back on sugary drinks and refined, processed foods, which may worsen oily scalp and fungal problems.

When to See a Doctor

Visit a dermatologist if hair fall lasts more than two to three months, you notice bald patches, the scalp is painful, very red or heavily scaled, or you also feel unusually tired, gain or lose weight suddenly, or have irregular periods. A simple blood test for iron, vitamin D and thyroid can reveal the real cause.

FAQs

Why does my hair fall more in summer?

Sweat, oil, sun damage, dehydration, scalp fungus and rough styling are the main reasons. Seasonal shedding and low nutrients can add to it.

Should I wash my hair daily in summer?

If you sweat a lot, yes, with a mild shampoo and gentle massage. A clean scalp helps control itching and fungus.

Is oiling good in summer?

Light oiling on dry lengths and ends is fine. Avoid heavy oil on a sweaty, oily or flaky scalp.

How long before I see improvement?

Shedding often reduces within a few weeks of fixing the routine, but new growth takes a few months to show.

Can sun really cause hair fall?

Strong sun weakens the hair shaft and causes breakage, which looks like hair fall. Covering your hair helps.

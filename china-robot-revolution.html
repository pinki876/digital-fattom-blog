China's Robot Revolution: Joe Hattab's Viral Video vs. The Fake Videos Fooling the Internet

Digital Fattom · AI & Technology

Millions have watched Joe Hattab's China video showing humanoid robots and driverless smart cities. But a separate set of "robot soldier" videos going viral on X turned out to be completely fake. Here's how to tell the difference — and what it actually means for the rest of us.

If you've been on social media lately, you've probably seen at least one video of a robot in China doing something that seems impossible. Some of these videos are real. Some are not. And the gap between the two says a lot about where AI actually stands in 2026 — and where our imagination has already run ahead of it.

The video that started it: Joe Hattab in China

Joe Hattab, the Dubai-based filmmaker known as one of the Arab world's biggest travel YouTubers, recently released a documentary-style video from China that racked up millions of views. It shows humanoid robots, AI-powered smart cities, driverless cars, and even palm-scan payment systems — technology that felt like science fiction just a few years ago, filmed as everyday reality on the streets of Chinese cities.

This video is real. It reflects genuine, rapid investment China has made in robotics and automation, visible to any traveler walking through its major cities today.

The videos that weren't: fake "robot soldiers"

Around the same time, a different kind of video went viral — this one showing humanoid robots in military training, armed with assault rifles, running obstacle courses at what looked like a PLA facility. One version alone crossed 1.9 million views on X.

These videos were AI-generated fakes. Fact-checkers traced visual inconsistencies between scenes that gave away the fabrication, once compared frame by frame. The robots looked real enough to fool most viewers, including seasoned social media users, at first glance.

So is China actually building a robot army?

Here's where it gets interesting: the fake videos were fake, but the underlying fear isn't baseless. China has genuinely been showcasing military robotics:

  • A four-legged combat robot called the PF-070, armed with anti-tank missiles, was unveiled as a "production-ready" platform for international sale.
  • The PLA has demonstrated a combat robot that mirrors a soldier's movements in real time using motion-sensing AI.
  • A robotics engineer working on China's humanoid programs told CBS News he believes true robot soldiers are still five to ten years away.

In other words: the viral rifle-training videos were staged by AI, not the PLA — but the technology gap between fiction and reality is closing faster than most people realize.

What this means for Pakistan and the rest of us

You don't need to live in China to feel the effects of this shift. Automation, AI-generated media, and robotics are moving fast enough that even everyday content — the videos in your feed — can no longer be trusted at face value. For Pakistan specifically, the bigger near-term question isn't robot soldiers; it's automation reshaping jobs in manufacturing, logistics, and customer service over the next decade.

The real skill worth building right now isn't fear of robots. It's media literacy — learning to pause, verify, and ask "is this even real?" before sharing.

Bilibili: China's YouTube Rival Just Went Global — What It Means for Pakistan

September 2026 · 5 min read

A Chinese video app most people outside Asia had never heard of just made a serious move on YouTube's territory. Bilibili — often called "China's YouTube" — has relaunched its international app, dropped one of its biggest sign-up barriers, and started actively recruiting creators outside China. If you make content for a living, this is worth paying attention to.

What exactly is Bilibili?

Bilibili launched back in 2009 and grew into one of China's largest video platforms, built around anime, gaming, comics, and a highly engaged young user base. It's not a small player: the company reports more than 350 million monthly active users, and in early 2026 people were spending close to two hours a day on the app on average. That's a level of engagement most platforms would kill for.

Why is it suddenly a global story?

Bilibili has relaunched its previously discontinued international app, and is reportedly building out an English-language website while hiring in markets like Japan, the US, and Europe. The single biggest change for creators: international users no longer need to submit a passport or ID just to upload content. That barrier kept a lot of non-Chinese creators away before — removing it opens the door much wider.

The platform has also been courting big-name Western creators. MrBeast started posting on Bilibili back in 2024 and has since built a following of roughly 8.8 million there, which signals the platform can pull serious international attention when it wants to.

A platform that already commands two hours of daily attention from 350 million people doesn't need to "arrive" quietly.

How is it different from YouTube?

  • Direct payment per view: Bilibili reportedly pays creators around $0.70 per 1,000 views directly, on top of ad revenue sharing, fan payments, livestreaming, and e-commerce income.
  • Niche-first content: Anime, gaming, and comics are the cultural core, whereas YouTube is built for everything.
  • Lower sign-up friction: No identity verification for international creators — a much lighter onboarding process than most global platforms.
  • Community depth over breadth: Bilibili's community, especially around donghua (Chinese animation), has historically been tightly knit and highly loyal, even when access was inconvenient.

What about Pakistan?

This part is still murky. Access from Pakistan has been inconsistent — some users report the app or site being blocked, others say it works intermittently. There's no official word yet on a formal Pakistan launch. What we do know is that Bilibili's global rollout strategy is deliberately staged: app first, English-language site next, market-by-market hiring after that. A Pakistan-specific push, if it comes, would likely follow that same pattern rather than arrive all at once.

Could it actually give YouTube a tough time?

Not overnight — but the ingredients are there. Bilibili already has a massive, highly engaged user base, a creator payment model that's more generous upfront than YouTube's ad-share-only approach, and a growing appetite to bring in creators YouTube has taken for granted. The obvious comparison people keep making is to TikTok's rise: a Chinese app expanding globally tends to trigger the same conversations around data security, content moderation, and government scrutiny, especially in Western markets. That friction could slow things down in some countries even as the platform grows fast in others.

For creators in Pakistan specifically, the realistic takeaway right now is: watch, don't wait. Access isn't stable yet, and there's no confirmed local monetization path. But a platform that pays directly per view and is actively trying to recruit creators is worth having on your radar for when — or if — it opens up properly.
